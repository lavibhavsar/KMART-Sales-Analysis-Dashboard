# KMART-Sales-Analysis-Dashboard
An interactive Excel dashboard analysing $3.19M AUD of Australian retail sales across 97 suburbs across all Australian states, 10 product categories and 2 channels (Store and Online). The goal was to answer three business questions: where does revenue come from, what is changing, and where should the business act?

Key Observations
Geography
NSW, VIC, QLD and WA generate 91.3% of sales. NSW alone contributes 36.6% of Sales whereas SA, NT, TAS and ACT combined contribute 8.7%.
Sales are concentrated at suburb level too as 22 of 97 suburbs deliver 50% of revenue. Top performers are Frankston ($105.7K), Campbelltown ($97.4K) and Chadstone ($84.2K).

Channel
Store drives 79.3% of sales ($2.53M) and Online 20.7% ($658K). Store also has a larger average sales transactions ($791 vs $503).
Online channel is growing faster: Jul-Dec 2025 Online sales rose 28.6% year on year vs 10.7% for Store.
Store dominance may reflect a preference to see and feel products before buying, but this is a hypothesis.

Categories
Five categories drive 81.1% of sales: Clothing & Fashion ($686K), Home & Living ($608K), Toys & Outdoor Play ($549K), Footwear ($382K) and Beauty & Personal Care ($360K).
The other five categories each contribute under 7%. Baby & Nursery category has the most transactions out of all categories (770) but has a lower average sales transactions value ($286).

Trend and seasonality
FY2024-25 is the highest financial year ($1.45M), but it is also the only full year in the data. FY2023-24 covers Jan-Jun 2024 and FY2025-26 covers Jul-Dec 2025, so financial years are not like-for-like.
On a like-for-like basis: Jan-Jun 2025 was down 21.3% on Jan-Jun 2024, while Jul-Dec 2025 was up 14.2% on Jul-Dec 2024. 2025 finished 4.7% below 2024.
Average transaction value typically sits at $650-$850 Thousands in Store and $275-$830 Thousands online. It spiked above $1,000 in Store in Jun 2024 ($1,035) and Aug 2025 ($1,156).
Aug 2025 was the best month ($207.8K), driven by bigger baskets rather than extra traffic: 198 transactions, in line with a normal month.
Weakest months were Nov 2024 ($92.6K) and Jun 2025 ($93.8K).

Risk
The top 10% of transactions generate 58% of revenue, so results are sensitive to a small number of large Home & Living orders. That category explains about 61% of the Jan-Jun 2025 decline.
Business Recommendations
Protect the Big 5 categories. Prioritise stock availability, range depth and promotions in Clothing, Home & Living, Toys, Footwear and Beauty, which carry 81% of revenue.
Defend the core four states and test growth elsewhere. Use low-cost pilots (online, click and collect) in SA, TAS and ACT before committing to new store investment.
Scale Online selectively. It is outgrowing Store, yet its share is just 7% in ACT, 10% in SA and 11% in QLD. Accessories, Electronics and Stationery already have the highest online share (23-27%).
Engineer the big-basket months. Review what drove Aug 2025 (promotions, stock, large Home & Living orders) and replicate it, while building campaigns for the Nov and Jun troughs.
Lift value in high-traffic, low-ticket categories. Use bundles and add-ons in Baby & Nursery, and review the range in Party & Gift Wrap and Accessories, which fell 39% and 36% in Jul-Dec 2025.
Strengthen the data foundation. Add margin, customer ID and returns data to move from revenue analysis to profitability and customer insight.
Data Notes
Revenue only: no cost or margin data, so profitability is not assessed.
26 transactions are exactly $1.00 and 9.7% are under $10; these may be returns or placeholders and should be validated.
Buyer maps one-to-one to Category, so the Buyer view repeats the Category view.
Online suburb attribution looks skewed (four suburbs hold about 39% of online sales), which may indicate fulfilment rather than customer locations.
